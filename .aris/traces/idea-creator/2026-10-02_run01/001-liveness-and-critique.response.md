1. The core framing — governance (manifests, egress policy, provenance audit) bolted around an existing MPC-GWAS stack (MAGE/HeIMDALL/MP-SPDZ lineage) — risks reading as policy plumbing rather than new foundations; the novelty threat is that all four ingredients trace to known work (declassification control à la Myers–Liskov, accountable-MPC/identifiable-abort, D-BASIS-style output-provenance auditing, and the Homer/Sankararaman/genomic re-identification measurement literature), so a PoPETs/CCS reviewer will demand a primitive or a theorem (e.g., a simulation-based definition of "reveal-manifest compliance" with a proof that enforcement composes across sequential releases) rather than an enforcement point and a dashboard.
2. The leakier problem is that GWAS *outputs themselves* are the channel — single-SNP association stats already enable membership and kinship inference, and cross-run release-relation auditing only helps if you can bound composition of many small reveals, which no released DP-style calculus for association statistics currently does; without a composition bound, the audit layer is forensic, not preventive, and the paper's security story collapses to "we log what leaked."
3. Biggest execution risk: the end-to-end burden — you must simultaneously implement enforced egress at the reveal primitive of a real MPC backend (across semi-honest and malicious settings, since governance in semi-honest-only is instantly discounted), then run *calibrated* genomic attacks (allele-frequency/kinship-accurate, realistic cohort sizes from 1000G/UKB-scale data, ideally consented multi-site data with IRB constraints) — each half alone is a full systems paper, and adversaries in half-baked leakage experiments get dismissed as toy.
4. The "named genomic attack models" requirement cuts both ways: naming them makes empirical claims falsifiable (good), but it invites exact-comparison against prior quantitative results (Sankararaman et al.'s likelihood-ratio attacks, regeneration attacks on aggregates), and if your enforced-egress system shows leakage numbers merely comparable to a cheap DP-noise or threshold-on-p-value baseline, reviewers will ask why MPC egress governance is needed at all — you need a case DP provably cannot cover (non-DP-reveals like raw p-value matrices, kinship matrices, cross-site joins).
5. Sharpest path to acceptance: pick one concrete governance gap with a formal statement — e.g., provable composition limits for a release graph of association-statistic reveals under a quantified adversary, with the manifest/egress machinery as the mechanism and the leakage study as the motivating measurement — and cut scope so the threat model (which sites collude? does the auditor see all reveals or only manifests?) is stated before any system code is written, because threat-model ambiguity is the first thing this venue's reviewers will attack.
